[p. 2-3] One Sunday evening, all the kids were in Wally’s yard.
Alice wanted to go to the park.
“Cool!” said Wally. “Let’s go!”
“Sorry,” said Tim, shaking his head.
“I have to finish writing Mom’s birthday card.”
“WHAT?” said Alice, “Mom’s birthday?”
“Yeah, you know it’s tomorrow, right?”
[p. 4-5] “Oh, uh, yes, of course,” Alice said, looking down.
Tim said goodbye and rode home to finish his card.
Alice yelled, “Quick! Help me! I need a card for Mom!”
Wally frowned. “The bookshops are already closed.”
“You could make one,” said Chuck, proud of himself.
“Excellent idea, Chuck, thanks!”
Alice felt much better.
[p. 6-7] Chuck invited her to his house to make the card there.
That way it could be a surprise for her mom.
The other kids went to the park, but Pam stayed to help Alice.
She always loved to make things.
[p. 8-9] “Make it super-big,
and decorate it with silver stars, hearts and yarn,” Pam suggested.
“Don’t be silly, there’s no time,” pointed out Chuck.
“Just do something easy, Alice.
What do you need?”
[p. 10-11] Alice thought.
“I need some colored pencils, glue and scissors.
How much green construction paper is there? Mom likes green.”
“There’s only a little,” Chuck said.
“There’s a lot of green paper. One side has been used,
but you could glue two pieces together. Then it would be stronger.”
[p. 12-13] Alice tried. Pam was right.
“OK, how many gold stickers are there? I need four.”
“There are a lot. I’ll get them for you.”
Pam got them.
“Is there any red glitter?”
“There’s some over here.” Pam went to get it.
[p. 14-15] Finally, the card was done. It looked great.
[p. 16-17] “Thanks, Chuck!” Alice smiled at him, but forgot to thank Pam.
Pam didn’t say anything, she just looked a little sad.
Chuck poked Alice, and she saw Pam’s face.
“Oh, right, thanks, Pam!”
[p. 18-19] The next day, Mom opened her cards and presents.
“Happy Birthday, Mom!” said Tim.
“What a lovely card!” Mom kissed him.
Alice was worried.
She just knew Mom wouldn’t like her card.
“Happy Birthday, Mom,” she said quietly.
“Alice! Did you make this?”
Alice turned red.
“I forgot to buy one, so I made one instead.
I’m sorry.”
[p. 20-21] “Don’t be sorry! I love it!
I love Tim’s card because I love elephants, but I love yours, too.
You spent time making it for me, and you recycled, too.
Well done. Thank you both!
OK, who wants a piece of birthday cake?”
