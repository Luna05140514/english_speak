[p. 2-3] Billy sits next to me in class.
He raises his hand in class . . . a lot!
I know, I know.
Everybody raises their hand in class.
It’s the number-one rule!
Sometimes you put up your hand to answer a question . . .
[p. 4-5] sometimes to ask a question . . .
and sometimes to share something interesting with the class.
[p. 6-7] But not Billy.
Billy was always raising his hand to tell on people.
Billy was always watching to see you break a rule.
Billy was a tattletale.
Last Monday, we had a test.
Sally whispered “Good luck” to me, and right away Billy raised his hand.
[p. 8-9] “Yes, Billy?” Mr. Fry asked.
“Mr. Fry, Sally is talking!”
Sally’s face turned bright red. She felt embarrassed.
Billy smiled. He felt proud.
“She just said ‘Good luck’ to me, Mr. Fry,” I said.
“Oh, well, that’s no big deal, Billy,” said Mr. Fry.
[p. 10-11] Last Wednesday in reading time, Tommy lifted his chair
and right away Billy quickly raised his hand.
“Yes, Billy?”
“Mr. Fry, Tommy is rocking in his chair!”
[p. 12-13] Tommy started to cry. He felt terrible.
Billy smiled. He felt proud.
“I’m sorry, Mr. Fry.
I was just pulling my chair closer to the table,” explained Tommy.
“Oh, well, that’s no big deal, Billy,” said Mr. Fry.
Last Friday, when Mr. Fry was explaining the homework,
I wrote my name on my new homework book.
Billy saw me writing and raised his hand right away.
[p. 14-15] “Yes, Billy?”
“Mr. Fry, Jenny is writing the answers in her book!”
My face turned blue. I felt so angry!
Billy smiled. I know he felt proud.
“Mr. Fry, that’s not true.
I was just writing my name on my new homework book, see?”
“Oh, well, that’s no big deal, Billy,” said Mr. Fry.
[p. 16-17] Twenty minutes ago, we started writing our journals in class.
Billy lost his pencil.
He saw me looking at him.
His face turned white like a ghost. He was scared.
Mr. Fry always said to be prepared for class.
“Are you going to tell Mr. Fry?” Billy asked me.
[p. 18-19] I felt sorry for him.
“Billy, it’s no big deal. Everybody forgets their pencil,” I said.
“You can borrow one of mine.”
“Jenny, are you talking over there when you should be writing in your journal?” Mr. Fry asked.
[p. 20-21] “Um . . . I’m sorry, Mr. Fry.
Jenny is just lending me a pencil,” Billy answered.
“Oh, well, that’s no big deal Billy,” said Mr. Fry.
Billy smiled and so did I.
I felt good.
I don’t think Billy will be a tattletale anymore.
