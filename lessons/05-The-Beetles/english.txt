[p. 2-3] One day, Tim found a book about beetles in the library.
He showed it to Wally, Paul and Pam.
They all thought the beetles were super cool.
Then Wally had a great idea.
“Let’s go to the forest and look for some more beetles.”
[p. 4-5] The kids rode their bikes along a path into the forest.
“Now what?” whispered Tim.
“Now we look for beetles,” answered Wally.
They looked under rocks.
They looked behind trees.
They looked in bushes. Nothing!
[p. 6-7] “I guess they’re hiding,” said Pam.
“Let’s wait for them to come out,” Wally suggested.
The children waited for a long time, but nothing happened.
“I can’t wait anymore,” said Paul,
“I’m going to look up in this tree.”
[p. 8-9] “Be careful!” called Pam.
But it was too late . . .
“Help!” shouted Paul,
“I’m stuck and I can’t get down!”
“We have to help him.” “It’s too high.”
“We need to work together,” said Wally.
Wally got down on his hands and knees,
Pam stood on his back,
and Tim got ready to catch Paul and Pam.
[p. 10-11] “Jump!” called Tim. “It’s only a little way.”
“Jump!” shouted Pam. “You can trust us.”
So Paul held Pam’s hands and fell right onto her.
Pam and Paul fell onto Tim,
and everyone landed on top of Wally.
[p. 12-13] Everyone was laughing until, suddenly, Paul said,
“Hey! What’s that by your head, Pam?”
“Wow! That’s a big bug!”
Pam screamed and hid behind a tree.
“Maybe it can bite . . .” she yelled.
“. . . and make you sick!” shouted Paul as he ran away.
[p. 14-15] “Don’t worry, that’s the long-armed scarab.
It can’t hurt you,” said Tim.
And then Wally said, “Look! There’s a beetle with a big nose.”
“Ahh, that’s a rhinoceros beetle,” said Tim.
“I think we bumped this old log,” began Wally.
“And that’s where the beetles were hiding,” said Tim.
[p. 16-17] They found all kinds of different beetles in the old log.
They found colorful jewel beetles,
scary stag beetles, and even some yellow long-horned beetles!
After a while, Paul said,
“Why don’t we catch a whole lot of beetles and take them home?”
“I think we should leave these beetles here in the forest,” said Wally.
“Then we can still see them whenever we want,” said Pam.
[p. 18-19] A few weeks later, the kids went back
and saw that the beetles had babies—lots of babies!
“Wow! I’m so glad we didn’t take the beetles home,” said Paul.
“Yeah, me too,” smiled Wally.
[p. 20-21] Beetles are insects.
They have a hard body, and six long thin legs.
They have four wings and like to eat rotten wood and leaves.
Beetles live in or under rotten logs in the forest.
They live most of their life under the ground,
and only come out of the ground a few months before they die.
The beetles we usually see are in the adult part of their life cycle.
The Life Cycle of Beetles:
Eggs become larvae.
Larvae become pupae.
Pupae become adults.
Adults lay eggs.
Look at all of the beetles that you can see in Taiwan.
Here are two special kinds of beetles:
Formosan long-armed scarab beetles are usually 50 to 60 mm long.
There are not many left in the forests,
so if you see one, don’t play with it.
Rhinoceros beetles can be 35 to 90 mm long.
They are very strong, and can lift 100 times their own weight.
That means they can lift 25 ten NT coins!
